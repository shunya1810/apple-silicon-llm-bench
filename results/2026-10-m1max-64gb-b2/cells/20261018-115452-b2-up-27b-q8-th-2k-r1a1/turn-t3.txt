"Offline and nominal records do not form distinct sensor classes. Because status repeats every five records, each status samples the same sawtooth sensor sequence, so offline values range widely just as nominal values do. For example, record 000003 has sensor=82.6 and zone=N61, while record 000018 has sensor=96.8 and zone=A46, and record 000000 has sensor=0.0 and zone=A10. Nominal records appear at indices 0, 5, 10, etc., and their zone suffixes fall by five: 10, 95, 90, 85, 80, 75, 70, 65, 60, 55, 50, 45, 40. Offline records appear at indices 3, 8, 13, etc., with suffixes 61, 56, 51, 46, 41, 36, 31, 26, 21, 16, 11, 96, 91. Their prefix alternates A and N because every fifth index flips parity. Thus the main difference is offset, not level: offline zones are shifted upward relative to nominal, and their sensor values are simply the ramp values at positions mod five equal to three rather than zero. No threshold separates the two statuses; both include near-zero and near-hundred sensor readings, and both include A and N zones."
