Records with an "offline" status consistently exhibit lower sensor values compared to those with a "nominal" status within the same cyclical sequence. In this dataset, the status sequence is fixed: nominal, degraded, recovering, offline, calibrating. Consequently, an "offline" record always follows a "recovering" record and precedes a "calibrating" one, placing its sensor value lower in the local decline trend than the "nominal" record that starts the next cycle or a previous one. For instance, Record 000003 (offline) has a sensor value of 82.6, while the subsequent cycle's Record 000005 (nominal) has a value of 71.2, which is lower, but this is a cross-cycle comparison. Within a single cycle, the "nominal" record (e.g., Record 000000, sensor 0.0) is the start of the drop, while "offline" (e.g., Record 000003, sensor 82.6) is the fourth step. Thus, "nominal" records represent the peak or reset point of the value cycle (or the lowest point depending on interpretation of the cycle start), whereas "offline" records are consistently in the lower-middle range of the descending slope. Regarding zones, both statuses share the same alternating A/N zone pattern and numeric suffix progression, meaning there is no distinct zone-based difference between the two statuses; the zone assignment is independent of the status type. For example, Record 000003 (offline, zone N61) and Record 000005 (nominal, zone N95) are in the same zone prefix family but different suffixes. Another comparison: Record 000008 (offline, A56) vs. Record 000010 (nominal, A90). Both are in 'A' zones. Finally, Record 000013 (offline, N51) vs. Record 000015 (nominal, N85) shows similar 'N' zone assignments. The primary difference remains the sensor value positioning within the decay cycle.
