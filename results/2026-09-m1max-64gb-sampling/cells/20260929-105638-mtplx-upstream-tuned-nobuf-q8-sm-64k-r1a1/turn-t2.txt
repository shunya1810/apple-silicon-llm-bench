Records with an "offline" status and those with a "nominal" status share identical structural behaviors regarding sensor values and zone assignments, differing only in their position within the recurring status cycle. Since the status follows a fixed five-record loop (nominal, degraded, recovering, offline, calibrating), "offline" records consistently appear at indices where $i \pmod 5 = 3$, while "nominal" records appear at $i \pmod 5 = 0$. Consequently, an "offline" record always has a sensor value that is three steps (1.4 units) lower than the immediately preceding "nominal" record in the same sawtooth cycle.

Regarding zones, both statuses are distributed evenly across the alternating 'A' and 'N' prefix system, but they occupy different specific positions within the 20-zone rotation. For instance, Record 000003 (offline) is in zone N61, while Record 000000 (nominal) is in zone A10. Similarly, Record 000008 (offline) is in zone A56, versus Record 000005 (nominal) in zone N95. Finally, Record 000013 (offline) is in zone N51, contrasting with Record 000010 (nominal) in zone A90. These examples confirm that while sensor values follow the global linear decay pattern, the specific zone assignment is tied to the absolute record index, not the status type.
