Records with an "offline" status consistently exhibit higher sensor values than those marked "nominal." This difference stems from the fixed status cycle, which repeats every five records. Because the sensor values generally decrease linearly across the dataset, the position of a status within this cycle dictates its typical value range. The "nominal" status appears first in each cycle (e.g., records 0, 5, 10), capturing the highest values in that group, while "offline" appears fourth (e.g., records 3, 8, 13), capturing lower values as the sensor reading has dropped further since the cycle start. Consequently, offline records tend to cluster in the lower-to-mid range of the 0-100 scale, whereas nominal records often sit near the peak of the local cycle.

Regarding zones, both statuses share the same alternating "A" and "N" prefix pattern, but specific zones are assigned based on the record index. For instance, **Record 000003** is offline in zone N61 with a sensor value of 82.6, while **Record 000000** is nominal in zone A10 with a value of 0.0 (an anomaly at the start). Comparing a mid-cycle example, **Record 000008** is offline in zone A56 with a value of 54.1, whereas the preceding **Record 000005** is nominal in zone N95 with a higher value of 71.2.
