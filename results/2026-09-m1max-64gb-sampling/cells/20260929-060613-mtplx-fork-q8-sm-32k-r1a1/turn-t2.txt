Records with an "offline" status do not occupy the same sensor value ranges as those with a "nominal" status due to the dataset’s periodic nature. Sensor values generally decay from high to low in a sawtooth pattern, but the status field cycles through five states (nominal, degraded, recovering, offline, calibrating) every five records. Consequently, "nominal" records appear at indices divisible by 5 (e.g., 0, 5, 10), while "offline" records appear at indices ending in 3 or 8 (e.g., 3, 8, 13). This means that within any single sensor decay cycle, "nominal" records capture a specific snapshot of the value, whereas "offline" records capture a different snapshot, typically lower in the cycle if the cycle starts high.

Regarding zones, there is no inherent difference in zone naming conventions between the two statuses; zones cycle independently of status. For example, **Record 000000** (nominal) has a sensor value of 0.0 in zone A10, which is an anomaly compared to the general high-start trend. In contrast, **Record 000003** (offline) has a sensor value of 82.6 in zone N61, and **Record 000005** (nominal) has a sensor value of 71.2 in zone N95. Comparing Record 000003 (offline, 82.6) with Record 000005 (nominal, 71.2) shows that offline records can have higher sensor values than nominal records when they occur later in a reset cycle, whereas Record 000000 is an outlier at the bottom of its cycle. The key difference is temporal positioning within the sensor cycle, not zone type.
