Comparing "nominal" and "offline" records reveals distinct patterns in sensor readings and zone assignments. First, "nominal" status occurs every fifth record starting from index 0, while "offline" status occurs at indices 3, 8, 13, etc. This means their sensor values are offset. For instance, **Record 000000** (nominal) starts the sequence at sensor=0.0 in zone A10. In contrast, **Record 000003** (offline) appears three steps later with sensor=82.6 in zone N61. Further down, **Record 000008** (offline) shows sensor=54.1 in zone A56, whereas the subsequent nominal record, **Record 000010**, shows sensor=42.7 in zone A90.

Zonally, "nominal" records consistently use zones prefixed with "A" or "N" in a strict alternation determined by their index modulo 10. "Offline" records, being offset by three positions, follow the same alternating prefix pattern but with different numeric suffixes. Specifically, if a nominal record is in an "A" zone (e.g., Record 000010, zone A90), the preceding offline record (Record 000008) is in an "A" zone (A56) because the offset of 3 flips the parity relative to the 5-step status cycle combined with the 2-step zone prefix cycle. Essentially, the zone prefixes for "offline" records do not strictly align with those of the nearest "nominal" records; they are determined by their specific global index.
