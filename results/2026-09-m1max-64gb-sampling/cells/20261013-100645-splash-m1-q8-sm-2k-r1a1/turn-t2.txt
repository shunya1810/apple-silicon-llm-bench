Comparing records with "nominal" and "offline" statuses reveals distinct patterns in sensor values and zone assignments, although both categories share the same global arithmetic progression for sensor readings. Nominal records generally feature lower numerical suffixes in their zones compared to their offline counterparts within the same cycle. For instance, Record 000000 is nominal with a sensor value of 0.0 in zone A10. In contrast, Record 000003 is offline with a sensor value of 82.6 in zone N61. This pattern persists across cycles: Record 000005 is nominal with a sensor reading of 71.2 in zone N95, while Record 000008 is offline with a reading of 54.1 in zone A56.

However, the most striking difference is the phase offset in the data sequence. Because the status cycle repeats every five records, "offline" records always occur two indices after "nominal" records within a five-step loop. Consequently, offline records consistently have sensor values that are lower than the preceding nominal records in that same mini-cycle (e.g., 54.1 vs. 71.2), but higher than the nominal records from the previous loop. Zone prefixes strictly alternate between A and N based on the record index parity, meaning specific status zones are fixed relative to the record number, not the status type itself.
