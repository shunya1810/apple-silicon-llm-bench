Records with an "offline" status and those with a "nominal" status exhibit distinct structural differences within the dataset's deterministic patterns. While both statuses follow the same linear decay in sensor values based on their record index, they occupy different positions in the five-cycle status loop. Specifically, "nominal" status corresponds to indices where the record number modulo 5 equals 0 (e.g., Record 000000), whereas "offline" status corresponds to indices where the record number modulo 5 equals 3 (e.g., Record 000003). Consequently, a "nominal" record always has a higher sensor value than the subsequent "offline" record three steps later in the cycle.

Regarding zones, both statuses appear in both "A" and "N" zones, but their assignment is fixed by the cycle position. For instance, Record 000000 is nominal in zone A10, while Record 000003 is offline in zone N61. Another comparison involves Record 001000 (nominal, zone A90) and Record 001003 (offline, zone N51). Finally, Record 002000 (nominal, zone A80) contrasts with Record 002003 (offline, zone N41). These examples illustrate that while the sensor values decay predictably across all records, the status and zone assignments are rigidly tied to the record index, preventing any random mixing of these attributes.
