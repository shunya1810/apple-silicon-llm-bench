Comparing "offline" and "nominal" records reveals distinct differences in their sensor value distributions, though both statuses cycle through the same range of values (0.0 to ~99.6). However, because the status rotates independently of the sensor value’s magnitude, any given status can appear at any point in the sawtooth cycle. Consequently, there is no inherent difference in the *range* of sensor values between the two groups; both contain high and low readings. The primary difference lies in their position within the 5-step status cycle relative to the sensor value decay.

Regarding zones, both statuses share the exact same pool of zone identifiers (A10, N27, etc.), which cycle independently.

Three concrete examples illustrate this:
1.  **Record 000003** is **offline** with a sensor value of **82.6** in zone N61.
2.  **Record 000000** is **nominal** with a sensor value of **0.0** in zone A10.
3.  **Record 000008** is **offline** with a sensor value of **54.1** in zone A56, while **Record 000005** is **nominal** with **71.2** in zone N95. These examples show that "offline" records do not exclusively have low values, nor do "nominal" records exclusively have high values; the status is decoupled from the sensor reading.
